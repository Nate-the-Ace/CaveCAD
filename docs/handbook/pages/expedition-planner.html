Expedition Planner

Plan a trip into the cave: pick the stations you want to reach, get a route, a time budget and a gear list, and build the callout card that stays with your topside contact. epl

When you reach for it

Before a trip, once you know which leads or stations the team is going for. Station Table is where to find them; this panel is where the trip itself is put together.

Opening it

Cave Survey > Expedition Planner. The panel is one page, with no tabs, laid out top to bottom in the same order as the printed callout card: Trip (start date, forecast place), Roster, Schedule (the days), Escalation (topside contact and what to do if there is no word), Route (the stops, pace, packing list, Plan trip and Save packet) and Card (Build card, with the plan's text underneath). Scroll down through it; fill it in as you go and nothing important gets skipped. Fields marked with a red * are required to build the card. It reads the drawing when it opens; after switching to another cave's tab, or after editing the survey, press Refresh at the bottom. The line beside it says how many stations the drawing has, or that it holds no survey.

Plan a trip

The Route section turns a few stations into a route, a time budget and a gear list, and writes it out as a page the team can print.

  1. Under Route, pick a station from the dropdown at the top, or type its name (the box finishes a name you start), and press Add stop. Do the same for every stop on the trip. A name that is not a station of this drawing is refused, with a note under the box saying so; adding a stop twice does nothing. Remove takes the selected stop off the list; Clear empties it.
  2. Press Plan trip. The route starts at the survey's first station, visits every stop in the shortest order it can find, and comes back to where it started. The text box at the bottom of the panel shows the directions leg by leg, the time in, at work and out, the distance, the rope for each pitch, and any warnings.
  3. Press Save packet to write trip-plan.html in the cave's folder, beside the drawing. Open it in a browser to read or print it. Save the drawing once first: an unsaved drawing has no folder to put it in.

The packet holds the objectives, the time budget, a sketch of the route over the cave's shape, the directions, what to watch for, the gear list and the team's packing list. It has no coordinates and no entrance location, so it can go to a team that has not been there before.

Highway-style directions

Underground you cannot tell which survey station you are standing at, but you can count the intersections you pass and judge how far you have walked. So on the callout card and the packet the directions count intersections. Under each leg's heading one line gives the whole leg: how many intersections, how far, and about how long (a leg with none says "straight through"). Then each intersection is a road sign, in the order you meet it: the small grey figure at the left is the walk to reach it (distance and time; only the distance when it takes under a minute), the arrow is which way to go, the large type says which intersection it is ("Intersection 3") and the small line under it says "of 7", how many passages meet there ("3-way") when the survey shows it, and a map ref. The map ref is the station's name, there only to find the spot on the route sketch: do not count on reading it in the cave. The last row is your stop. Junctions only a few feet apart are really one wiggle, so they count as one intersection, showing the net turn. Turns are drawn relative to the way you were already going (straight, slight, left or right, sharp, or back the way you came); a pitch out of an intersection shows a vertical arrow, and after a pitch the sign shows the compass heading instead. A leg with no intersections is one line: follow the passage to the station. Pitches are covered by the rope list, not the signs. The plan text in the panel follows the same structure: each leg's totals, then one numbered line per intersection ("Walk 251 ft, then at the 1st intersection (3-way, map ref A11) go slight left"), then where you arrive.

Pace

Walking time is worked out at 264 feet a minute, a 3 mph hiking pace, plus time on rope for each pitch and 20 minutes of work at each stop. Cave passage is usually slower than a trail: type your own pace in feet per minute in Walking pace and press Plan trip again. Leave it blank to go back to 264. The pace and the packing list are saved in stations.json, so the whole team plans with the same numbers.

What it cannot tell you

  • The route follows the survey line. It is not a guarantee that the way is safe or easy: crawls, water, climbs and loose ground are only known where someone wrote them in the survey notes.
  • Rigging is not read from the map yet. Every pitch says "rig not on map, confirm": count the anchors and hangers from what you know of the cave before you pack.
  • A stop that cannot be reached along the surveyed line is listed as a warning and left out of the route. A shot whose walls say the passage is under 3 feet high or wide is flagged as tight.

Callout card

Build card, in the Card section at the bottom, writes callout-card.html beside the drawing: two printed sheets for a trip. It builds nothing until every required item is there, and then says Missing: with all of the gaps at once, not just the first. Required:

  • the start date;
  • at least one person on the roster, while Include roster on the card is ticked;
  • at least one day in the schedule;
  • the topside contact's name, the contact phone, and the if no word by callout line;
  • at least one stop under Route.

The forecast place and the callout buffer are optional (the buffer defaults to 120 minutes). A field holding only spaces counts as empty.

  • Sheet 1, for topside: the roster (name, role, squeeze limit, medical notes, emergency contact), the schedule, who to call if there is no word, and the forecast for each trip day.
  • Sheet 2, for the team: the route sketch, turn-by-turn directions, hazard notes found on the way, and the rope list.
  • Schedule: a start date, then one row per day with an entry time, work hours, and a night: out (back on the surface) or camp (stay underground). Turnaround is entry plus the way in (on days that start from the surface) plus work hours. Callout is the expected time out plus your buffer. Camp nights have no callout. Only the trip's first way in and last way out use the route's times; moves between camps are not timed.
  • Forecast: looked up when you build, from the drawing's location rounded to about 10 km, or from a town you type. The exact entrance is never sent and never printed. With no network the card says so and still builds.
  • Rain plus water: if a day's forecast is wet and a station note on the route mentions water, the card carries a bold warning. It says "check", never "safe".
  • Privacy: the roster and phone numbers are saved on this computer only, not in the cave's folder, so Drive does not carry them. Untick Include roster for a copy that leaves your hands.

Where it is saved

Pace, the packing list and the trip's days are saved in stations.json in the cave's folder, the same file Station Table keeps its marks in, so Google Drive carries them to the team. Every save reads the file again first and changes only these settings, so a teammate's marks are never written over. The packet and the card are written beside the drawing as trip-plan.html and callout-card.html. The roster and phone numbers stay on this computer. Save the drawing once first: an unsaved drawing has no folder to keep any of this in.

The panel follows the drawing it was opened on. If you switch to another cave and press Plan trip, it reads the new drawing, clears the stops, and asks you to pick them again.
